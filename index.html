How to prepare

  1. Look back through your exercise book at the tasks and questions from your lessons.
  2. Missed a lesson? Ask your teacher, or someone in your class, what you missed and catch up.
  3. Read the Revision tab and do the quiz at the end of each part.
  4. Use BBC Bitesize KS3 History and Oak National Academy KS3 History.

To get the very top marks

  1. Answer every multiple choice question. Never leave one blank: take your best guess.
  2. Explain WHY and HOW, not just dates. Explain why something happened, and how it affected people or events.
  3. Use PEEL for every paragraph, including the Section C essay (see the example below). For change and continuity, add a paragraph that starts However, and finish with a judgement.

PEEL example: Why did fascism rise in Europe?

Point: Fascism rose because many people were unhappy after the First World War.

Evidence: Italy experienced economic problems, unemployment and political instability after 1918.

Explain: People lost faith in democracy and wanted a strong leader who promised to restore national pride and order.

Link: Therefore, post-war problems helped fascist leaders gain support.

  1. Section C essay: use PEEL. Point: say what changed or stayed the same. Evidence: give a fact or date. Explain: why it matters. Link back to the question. Then write a paragraph that starts However, and finish with a judgement.

Part 1

Why did Manchester pay for a statue of Emmeline Pankhurst?

Part A: The suffrage movement

Who was Emmeline Pankhurst?

  • Born in Manchester in 1858.
  • A leading campaigner for women’s right to vote.
  • Founded the Women’s Social and Political Union (WSPU) in 1903.
  • Famous for the slogan “Deeds, not words”.
  • Supported militant protest to get attention for women’s suffrage.

Why was a statue built?

  • To celebrate her contribution to women’s voting rights.
  • To recognise Manchester’s important role in the suffrage movement.
  • To remember the women who fought for political equality.
  • To inspire future generations to stand up for their rights.
  • To give women a greater presence in public monuments, which have mostly celebrated men.
Key argument“The statue recognises Pankhurst as an important historical figure who helped change British democracy and fought for women’s political rights.”

Part 2

Why is there a statue of Alice Hawkins in Leicester?

Part A: The suffrage movement

Who was Alice Hawkins?

  • A suffragette from Leicester.
  • Worked as a shoe machinist in a Leicester factory.
  • A member of the WSPU, campaigning for women to have the vote.
  • Took part in protests and was arrested several times.

Why does she have a statue?

  • She represents ordinary working women in the suffrage movement.
  • She campaigned for women’s rights in Leicester and nationally.
  • Her story shows the movement was not just led by wealthy or famous women.
  • It celebrates women’s courage, determination and activism.
  • It makes sure Leicester remembers its part in the fight for equality.
Key argument“Alice Hawkins is remembered because she represents ordinary working-class women who risked their freedom and safety to fight for the right to vote.”

Part 3

What does Rosa May Billinghurst’s story reveal?

Part A: The suffrage movement

Who was Rosa May Billinghurst?

  • A British suffragette with a disability affecting her mobility.
  • She used a modified tricycle or wheelchair to get around.
  • A member of the WSPU, who took part in demonstrations and militant protests.
  • She was arrested and imprisoned for her campaigning.

What does her story reveal?

  • Women with disabilities played an important role.
  • Women were determined: she kept campaigning despite physical difficulties and the risk of arrest.
  • The movement used militant methods: direct action, protests and civil disobedience.
  • Women faced discrimination because of their sex and disability.
  • The movement was diverse: women from different backgrounds fought for the same right.

Important suffrage dates

Remember: The 1918 Act gave the vote to some women. The 1928 Act gave women the same voting rights as men.
Key argument“Billinghurst’s story reveals that the suffrage movement involved women from different backgrounds, and that many were prepared to overcome considerable barriers to fight for equality.”

Part 4

Why did the Treaty of Versailles bury problems rather than solve them?

Part B · Signed 28 June 1919

1. Germany felt humiliated

  • Germans believed the treaty was unfair.
  • They had not been allowed to negotiate the terms.
  • Many Germans wanted revenge.

2. Economic problems

  • Reparations were a huge financial burden.
  • Germany struggled with debt and economic instability.
  • This added to resentment towards the Allies.

3. A weak League of Nations

  • Created to keep the peace.
  • But it had no army of its own.
  • It struggled to enforce its decisions.

4. New borders caused tension

  • Germany lost land, and new countries were created.
  • Some ethnic groups ended up in countries they did not identify with.

5. Hitler used the treaty to gain support

  • He promised to overturn the treaty.
  • He promised to rebuild Germany’s military.
  • He promised to restore German pride and win back lost land.

Model assessment answer

Question: Why did the Treaty of Versailles bury problems rather than solve them?

The Treaty of Versailles buried problems rather than solving them because it created resentment in Germany. For example, Germany was forced to accept the war guilt clause and pay £6.6 billion in reparations. This made many Germans feel humiliated and angry towards the Allies. As a result, extremist leaders such as Hitler were able to gain support by promising to overturn the treaty. Therefore, instead of creating lasting peace, the treaty helped create tensions that contributed to the outbreak of the Second World War.

Part 5

Why was there a rise in fascism in the 1920s and 1930s?

Part B

What is fascism?

  • An extreme right-wing political idea that supports:
  • A powerful dictator or leader.
  • Extreme nationalism and national pride.
  • Strong military forces, and obedience to the state.
  • Opposition to democracy, control of the media, and crushing opponents.

Five reasons fascism became popular

1

Anger after WW1

  • Millions had died in the war.
  • Countries faced economic and political problems.
  • People wanted strong leaders to restore national pride.
2

The Treaty of Versailles

  • Many Germans felt they were treated unfairly.
  • Hitler promised to reverse the treaty.
3

Fear of communism

  • The wealthy and business owners feared a communist revolution.
  • Fascists promised to stop communism and protect property.
4

Economic problems

  • Unemployment and poverty grew.
  • Fascist leaders promised jobs, stability and recovery.
5

Weak democratic governments

  • Governments struggled to solve big problems.
  • Fascists promised quick decisions and strong leadership.

Model assessment answer

Question: Why was there a rise in fascism in Europe in the 1920s and 1930s?

One reason fascism rose was because of economic problems. Many people were unemployed and living in poverty, so they lost confidence in democratic governments. Fascist leaders such as Hitler promised jobs, stability and a stronger country. Another reason was fear of communism, as some people believed fascist governments would protect them from a communist revolution. Therefore, economic hardship, fear and political instability helped fascist leaders gain support.

Part 6

How did the Great Depression impact Europe?

Part B · From 1929

What was the Great Depression?

  • A worldwide economic crisis that began in 1929 after the Wall Street Crash in the USA.
  • It caused major economic difficulties all through the 1930s.

1. Unemployment increased

  • Factories and businesses closed.
  • Millions of people lost their jobs.
  • Families struggled to afford food and housing.

2. Poverty increased

  • People struggled to pay bills, and living conditions got worse.
  • Many families depended on government support or charity.

3. Businesses failed

  • Trade went down and factories made fewer goods.
  • Businesses went bankrupt.

4. Political extremism increased

  • People became unhappy with democratic governments, and some turned to extremist parties.
  • Hitler gained support by promising to fix Germany’s economy.

5. Democracy became weaker

  • Governments struggled to deal with the crisis.
  • People lost faith in politicians, and some countries became more authoritarian.

Why was Germany hit so hard?

  • Germany had borrowed large amounts of money from the USA in the 1920s.
  • When the Depression began, American banks demanded their loans back.
  • German businesses lost money and unemployment rose dramatically.
  • Support for Hitler and the Nazi Party grew.

Model assessment answer

Question: How did the Great Depression impact Europe?

The Great Depression had a serious impact on Europe because unemployment and poverty increased. Many businesses and factories closed, meaning millions of people lost their jobs. Germany was particularly badly affected because it relied heavily on American loans. This economic crisis caused people to lose faith in democratic governments and increased support for extremist leaders such as Hitler, who promised to create jobs and rebuild Germany.

Remember: Key statistic: around 6 million Germans were unemployed by 1932.

Part 7

What role did appeasement have in causing WW2?

Part C: The causes of the Second World War

What was appeasement?

  • The policy, used by Britain and France, of giving Hitler some of what he wanted, hoping this would prevent another war.
  • The British Prime Minister Neville Chamberlain was its main supporter.

Why did Britain and France use it?

  • They wanted to avoid another devastating war: WW1 had killed millions.
  • Britain was not fully prepared for war.
  • Some felt Germany had been treated too harshly at Versailles.
  • They hoped Hitler would stop if some demands were met.

How did appeasement help cause WW2?

  • Hitler grew more confident because his earlier actions were not stopped.
  • Germany became stronger through military expansion.
  • Hitler realised Britain and France were reluctant to fight.
  • The Munich Agreement encouraged him to keep expanding.
  • In March 1939 he took the rest of the Czech lands, showing he could not be trusted.

Was appeasement a good idea?

Arguments FORArguments AGAINST
It helped Britain prepare for warIt encouraged Hitler to become more aggressive
It avoided war in 1938It allowed Germany to become stronger
It gave Britain time to rearmIt abandoned Czechoslovakia to German demands
Another war would have been devastatingIt failed to prevent war in 1939

Model assessment answer

Question: What role did appeasement have in causing WW2?

Appeasement helped cause the Second World War because it encouraged Hitler to believe that Britain and France would not stop his expansion. For example, in 1938, the Munich Agreement allowed Hitler to take the Sudetenland from Czechoslovakia. This made Hitler more confident and encouraged him to continue expanding. In March 1939, he took control of the rest of the Czech lands, showing that he could not be trusted. Therefore, appeasement contributed to the outbreak of war because it failed to stop Hitler’s aggression.

Part 8

Why was the ‘Great War’ not the war to end all wars?

WW1: 1914 to 1918

What was the Great War?

  • Another name for the First World War (1914 to 1918).
  • Many hoped it would be the “war to end all wars”, because its destruction would stop future conflicts.
  • But another world war began just 21 years later.

Six reasons the Great War did not end all wars

1

Versailles caused resentment

  • Germany felt humiliated.
  • Reparations caused economic difficulties.
  • Hitler used anger about the treaty to gain support.
2

The League of Nations was weak

  • It was set up to prevent war, but had no army.
  • It struggled to stop aggressive countries.
3

The rise of dictators

  • Hitler in Germany, Mussolini in Italy, and others.
  • They encouraged nationalism and military expansion.
4

Economic problems

  • The Great Depression caused unemployment and poverty.
  • People lost faith in democracy, and extremists gained support.
5

Aggression from Germany, Italy and Japan

  • Italy invaded Abyssinia in 1935.
  • Japan invaded Manchuria in 1931 and China in 1937.
  • Germany expanded its territory.
6

Appeasement failed

  • Britain and France let Hitler break agreements.
  • He grew more confident, and invaded Poland in 1939.

Model assessment answer

Question: Why was the Great War not the war to end all wars?

The First World War was not the war to end all wars because the peace settlement created further problems instead of preventing future conflict. For example, the Treaty of Versailles made Germany feel humiliated and angry because it had to accept war guilt and pay reparations. This helped Hitler gain support by promising to make Germany powerful again. Furthermore, the League of Nations was too weak to stop aggressive countries. Therefore, unresolved problems after the First World War contributed to the outbreak of the Second World War in 1939.
